Understanding Voltage Drop

Every electrical conductor has inherent resistance. As current flows through a wire over a distance, some electrical energy is converted into heat, resulting in a loss of voltage at the load end. Excessive voltage drop can cause motors to overheat, lights to dim, and sensitive equipment to malfunction.

The Formulas Used

Single-Phase / DC Circuits:

Vd = (2 * L * I * rho) / A

Three-Phase Circuits:

Vd = (sqrt(3) * L * I * rho) / A

Where:

  • Vd = Voltage Drop in Volts
  • L = Length of the one-way circuit in meters
  • I = Load current in Amps
  • rho = Conductor resistivity (Copper $\approx 0.01724$, Aluminum $\approx 0.0282$)
  • A = Cross-sectional area of the cable in mm²

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I reduce voltage drop?

You can reduce voltage drop by increasing the cable cross-sectional area (using a thicker wire), shortening the cable run length, or lowering the load current.
